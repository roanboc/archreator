# Business-model discovery

Use this route when the subject is an organization, business line, service
line or operating model. The business itself is being modeled: its customers,
offers, economics and way of creating value. Do not use it merely because a
solution is large. For one application or technical initiative, start with
strategy discovery and model only the business context the solution consumes.

Where useful business-design content already exists, refresh it rather than
starting over. Preserve facts that still hold, remove obsolete ones and focus
the conversation on evidence that conflicts or is missing.

## Inputs

- the model boundary and level: enterprise, domain or solution;
- existing business-design documents and evidence in scope;
- the language customers and operators use for the business; and
- known products, services, customer groups, partners and revenue or funding
  arrangements.

Evidence may come from observable operations, existing documents or a named
person who is accountable for the subject. A typical business is not evidence.

## Discovery sequence

Work with one customer segment and one offer at a time. Ask in small groups,
reflect the emerging model in ordinary language and consolidate overlapping
answers before assigning identifiers.

1. **Find the real customer groups.** Ask who pays, who uses, who decides and
   who would notice first if the offer disappeared. These may be different
   people. Separate segments only when their jobs, pains, gains or buying
   behaviour materially differ.
2. **Understand their jobs.** Ask what each segment is trying to achieve,
   including the functional job, how success affects them socially or
   emotionally, and what they do today instead.
3. **Name pains and gains.** Ask what creates cost, delay, risk, effort or a
   failed outcome; then what is required, expected or genuinely valuable.
   Capture the measure or evidence of severity where one exists. Two pains
   that differ only in degree are one pain with segment-specific severity.
4. **Describe the offer and test its fit.** Name products and services as a
   customer would. Connect each material pain to a pain reliever and each
   material gain to a gain creator. An unmatched pain or gain is a real gap or
   an explicit choice not to serve it, never a reason to invent filler.
5. **Describe the business model per genuinely different offer.** Explore
   customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships,
   revenue or funding, key activities, key resources, key partners and costs.
   Tiers with the same economics may share a model; offers with different
   customers or economics normally do not.
6. **Make delegated work visible.** Ask whether a human, an AI system or a
   hybrid arrangement performs or assists each important activity. For an AI
   or hybrid actor, capture autonomy, concrete decision rights and escalation
   to a named role.
7. **Prepare strategy without re-interviewing.** Carry forward the supported
   stakeholders, drivers, outcomes, capabilities, resources and value stages
   as inputs to strategy discovery. Principles still need to be discovered
   directly because a canvas does not determine them.

## Judgement tests

- A new segment earns its own view only when it changes the value proposition
  or how the offer reaches and sustains that customer.
- A new product earns its own business model only when its customer,
  economics, delivery model or partners materially differ.
- A proposed element survives only when a source or accountable person can
  support it.
- A canvas is useful only when it exposes fit, a decision or a material gap.
  Do not create one to make the model look complete.
- Ask a person to resolve something only when conflicting evidence or missing
  meaning would materially change the business model. Present the minimum
  facts, viable interpretations, consequences and a recommendation.

## Outputs

Create `architecture/0_business-design/` only when business design is in
scope and there is supported content. Keep the Value Proposition Canvas and
Business Model Canvas as diffable Markdown tables; add a compact visual only
when it makes fit or the nine-block business model easier to understand.

Record:

- one supported customer profile per materially distinct segment;
- one value map per materially distinct offer;
- the connections between jobs, pains, gains, relievers and creators;
- one business model per genuinely different product or service; and
- explicit gaps, inconsistent evidence and sources beside the affected facts.

Update `architecture/README.md` with the local content, boundary and any
business-design context owned by another model. Do not create empty canvas
files, generic pending rows or a mandatory approval record.

## Anti-patterns

- Filling a block from what a business of this kind usually does.
- Asking in canvas terminology when a business question is clearer.
- Interviewing all segments and products in parallel until their facts blur.
- Producing long, overlapping catalogues instead of consolidating them.
- Treating every pricing tier as a separate product without testing economics.
- Deriving strategy from unsupported canvas content.
- Recording personal judgements about participants rather than observable
  constraints, decisions or disagreements.
