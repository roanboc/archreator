Read it instead

An AI agent can build almost anything you ask for. But it only knows what you told it. Where you left a gap, it fills it with a guess, and keeps going. So you get a working version of the wrong thing.

ArChreator closes that gap first. It turns your idea into a picture of your business that you can check, before anything is built.

Here is how it goes. You say the idea, in your own words. ArChreator works out what it means for your business: why you want it, who does what, and what you need to know. Then it shows you a picture of what changes, and asks one question at a time. You answer, or you correct it. Round and round, until it reads true. Then you confirm. Your confirmation is what makes it true. Now it hands over: a short note of the change, the software that is needed, and your rules. Your own delivery framework designs the software. A coding agent builds it, and tests it. If the design, or a test, shows that you meant something else, that comes back to you. It is not settled in the code. Checks confirm the picture still matches what was built. You review once, and merge. You end up with two things: the software, and a description of your business that you confirmed. That second one lasts.

All of it lives in your repository, as plain text. One file is the front door. Every agent reads it first. The architecture folder holds the picture, in numbered folders, in the order the questions are asked. One: why the business exists. Two: who does what. Three: what it needs to know. Four: what software does it. Five: where it runs. Beside them, one short note for every change: what changed, why, and who confirmed it. Three small checks keep it honest. Previews and briefs are made when you ask, and thrown away.

ArChreator is free and open source. Install it in your coding agent, and say your first idea.

You ask

Where does it all live?

In your repository, in folders numbered in the order the questions are asked. A folder appears when there is something to put in it. Pick one.

  • your-project/

AGENTS.md

The front door for any agent

Every coding agent reads this file first. It says who decides, how much of the business is described, and which delivery framework builds the software.

In the bike shopThe owner decides. The shop is described as one small business. No delivery framework yet.

architecture/README.md

The front page of your business

One line per folder below: filled in, kept somewhere else, left out on purpose, or a gap you know about. Nothing is silently missing.

In the bike shopStrategy, business and information are filled in. Where it runs is left out: nothing depends on it.

architecture/0_business-design/

Who it is for, and how it pays its way

Two one-page canvases: your customers and what you offer them, and how the business works as a whole. Used when you describe a whole business, skipped for a single application.

In the bike shopPeople who ride to work and cannot be without their bike for long.

architecture/1_strategy/

Why it exists

Your goals, what presses on you, the principles you will not break, and what the business must be good at. Every later change is judged against this.

In the bike shopNobody waits on the phone. A customer is asked for their details once.

architecture/2_business/

Who does what

What you offer, who does the work, in which steps, under which rules. The people who use an AI agent and the agents themselves are both written down, with what each may decide alone.

In the bike shopRepair booking, done by the front desk. A text is sent only to a customer who agreed to it.

architecture/3_information/

What it needs to know

The information the business runs on, what each piece holds, and who uses it.

In the bike shopCustomer: name, phone number, and whether they agreed to be texted.

architecture/4_application/

What software does it

A list, one line per piece of software: what it serves, where it is specified, which code it is. The design itself belongs to your delivery framework, and this line points to it.

In the bike shopThe booking page serves Repair booking. Not built yet, and it says so.

architecture/5_technology/

Where it runs

Only where the business cares: a law about where data is kept, a hosting contract, who pays.

In the bike shopEmpty. Nothing about the shop depends on where its software runs.

architecture/scope/

One short note per change

What changed, why, what was left out on purpose, and who confirmed it. Numbered in order, so the history of your decisions reads like a diary.

In the bike shop1. Book a repair online.

architecture/decisions/

A single call, written down

One decision that matters but is smaller than a change, kept so nobody argues it again. Most often it is how much an AI agent may decide by itself.

In the bike shopThe text sender may text a customer without asking the front desk.

architecture/relationships.md

What connects to what

Every connection between two things, written once. It is what lets a tool answer the question that matters before a change: what else does this touch?

In the bike shopThe booking page serves Repair booking.

scripts/

Three checks you can run yourself

They need nothing installed and no network. Every link leads somewhere, every reference names something real, and every page talks about your business.

On every changeAll three run before anything is merged.

.archreator/work/

Throwaway pages

Previews of a change, a brief that answers one question, a PDF for somebody who will never open the repository. Made on request and never saved. Delete the folder and nothing is lost.

In the bike shopThe preview page above was written here.

You ask

Does it replace the tools I use?

No. It runs inside your coding agent and hands over to your delivery framework. It designs no software and builds none.

It runs inside

  • Claude Code
  • GitHub Copilot
  • Codex CLI
  • Gemini CLI

One method, the same in each. A person can follow the same steps by hand: an agent helps, and is never the only way in.

It hands over to

  • Spec Kit
  • OpenSpec
  • Kiro
  • BMAD
  • arc42 or C4
  • your own design notes

Whichever you use writes the technical design. ArChreator keeps the list of where each part of it is written, and checks that the list is whole.

You ask

How do I start?

In Claude Code, two commands install it and a third sets up your project.

/plugin marketplace add roanboc/archreator
/plugin install archreator@archreator
/archreator:establish-project
  1. Install it in your agent. Copilot, Codex and Gemini have their own steps.
  2. Say what the project is. It asks, you answer, and thirteen small files land in your repository.
  3. Say your first idea.
